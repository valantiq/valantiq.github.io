Seven questions decide whether a fund will give your data a try.

Funds judge data differently from marketers. These are their questions, and knowing them in advance can multiply what the data earns.

Does it map to listed companies?

A fund invests in securities, so the data has to connect to them.

Brands need linking to their parent companies and their tickers. An ecommerce data provider tracks products. A fund needs those products rolled up to the listed consumer goods company that owns them.

How much history is there?

Funds test a dataset against past results before they trust it.

Several years of consistent history is far more valuable than a richer dataset that began last spring. An advertising spend provider that has observed campaigns since long before the fund came calling has an advantage that cannot be bought later.

How wide is the coverage?

Coverage means companies, categories and countries.

A web & app usage data provider covering every significant website and app in its market answers more questions than one covering a single sector. We value global datasets that serve the widest range of strategies, and niche single-market or single-category datasets that suit funds with new strategies.

How granular and stable is it?

Funds prefer the most detailed view you can lawfully provide, delivered the same way every time.

Methodology changes matter. If how the data is collected or measured shifts, the fund needs to know when and how, or it cannot tell a real trend from a measurement artefact.

How fresh is it?

A signal is worth most before the market has it.

Daily delivery is the standard funds look for. Weekly is still useful, but a step down. A provider that can show what’s changing within days is describing the present. A quarterly summary describes the past.

Who else has it?

Exclusivity impacts price.

This is why the choice between exclusive, limited and broad distribution is one of the most important commercial decisions a data owner makes, and why it should be made deliberately rather than by accident.

Will it pass compliance?

Every fund’s compliance team asks four questions.

Where does the data come from? Do you have the right to license it? Does it contain personal data? Could it contain inside information? A clear answer to each, prepared in advance, is often the difference between a trial that starts in weeks and one that never starts.

Licensing your data leaves your core business safe.

Your existing client agreements set the limits, and four safeguards keep you within them.

They are checked for whether they allow this use, or would need updating with your clients. Even if they allow it, you can still decide not to go ahead, if it could put your reputation or core business at risk.

Funds receive aggregated, anonymised outputs. Individual-level or household-level data is aggregated or it is not used at all.

A fund does not compete with your core business, and it licenses data for its own edge, so it has no reason to share it. Its licence also bars it from passing the data on, and Valantiq never holds or resells it.

Funds are institutional buyers whose compliance teams examine every dataset before it is used. Their standard is usually stricter than marketing’s. Data that is fully compliant in marketing can still fall short. That scrutiny protects you too.
